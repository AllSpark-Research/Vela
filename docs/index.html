Intelligence for serving users in real-world scenarios

Serving users in real-world scenarios requires models to learn from context, identify relevant signals in noisy behavior histories, integrate evidence over time, and adapt to changing preferences.

Vela explores user behavior as supervision: using observed behavior to build tasks that teach LLMs to understand preferences, intent, and likely actions. The benefits extend to general capabilities and subsequent reinforcement learning.

Based on anonymized histories from 100,000 users, the framework constructs 100,000 candidate tasks across 91 templates and retains approximately 75,000 training examples.

A stronger 35B model

Fine-tuning Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on Vela-Instruct produces Vela-35B-A3B. It improves over the base model on all ten evaluated benchmarks.

On VelaBench, the model reaches 63.0, compared with 51.5 for the base model and 62.3 for Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B. This comparison concerns the user-understanding benchmark.

From behavior to supervision

Vela organizes six kinds of behavior into evidence-grounded tasks: views, searches, purchases, posts, follows, and dislikes. Predictive questions use observed future behavior as ground truth. Open and Attribution questions use task-specific rubrics.

More behavior data, stronger LLM capabilities

Increasing Vela-Instruct from 4.7K to 75K examples raises VelaBench scores from 59.2 to 63.0 and the independent user-related average from 43.5 to 47.5. General benchmarks remain above the base model at every tested scale.

A better foundation for RL

With the same reinforcement-learning data and settings, first training on Vela-Instruct leads to better final results than applying RL directly to the base model.

After RL, Vela-based models reach 66.2 on VelaBench and 37.6 on ProfileSummary, compared with 58.5 and 29.9. Both gains are 7.7 points.
